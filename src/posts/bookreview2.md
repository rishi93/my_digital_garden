# One Arranged Murder - Chetan Bhagat

## Thoughts

So true to my promise after reading Chetan Bhagat's last book, and being buoyed by the success of having finished one of his earlier books, I have indeed read another book from start to finish. And this one happens to one of his too, the sequel to his previous book that I read.

## Review (without spoilers)

This one is again a whodunit, and the two protagonists from the previous book continue their lives in this one. The method of the crime in this one is indeed quite dramatic. The characters are all from one big joint family and the family drama and the background stories are well thought out and make for an interesting read. But the plotline in this one feels a bit weak compared to the previous book by the author. And there are a few glaring gaps in the plot here as well.

How do you get invited to someone's engagement after you just called them "a fat cow" in front of their fiance. And also there's the error where the two characters say they meet the detectives and then go the police, and just two pages later, it's the other way around. For an observant reader, these kind of "plotholes" are offputting. I'm not sure if "plotholes" is a word, it's just one that I made up. It's like potholes but for the plot, get it?

## Review (with spoilers)

The method of crime is eerily similar to the previous book, where the culprit manages to fool everyone with their seemingly tight alibi which eventually is uncovered by the protagonist. Just this fact sort of dampens the final review for anyone who's read the previous book.

And another fact that leaves one with their head scratching is: How are the clues that are staring smack bang right at us in the scene of the second murder ignored by everyone? I mean if a murder victim is found with hickeys and love bites all over their body, I assume this raises some flags and should've led the police to perform DNA analysis here as well, right? Especially since they do it for the first murder. If they did, it would've led them straight to the murderer, and the story would've ended much sooner. But we have to endure this gross misjudgement and the story moves on, with this particular fact appreciated much later towards the end.
